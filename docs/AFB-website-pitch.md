# One address. Three doors.

### What your website should be doing for American Food & Beverage

---

## The problem, plainly

Three groups of people look up American Food & Beverage online before they ever speak to you:

**A buyer** deciding whether to put your nuts on their shelf.
**A candidate** deciding whether to apply for your open driver position.
**Your own crew** trying to find out what's on today's run.

All three land in the same place. And right now that place doesn't tell any of them what they
came to find out.

You have a 60,000 square foot SQF-certified plant, three brands, your own fleet, and 370
products you roast, blend and pack yourself. None of that is visible in the first ten seconds.

---

## Door one — Customers

**The buyer who's never heard of you.**

They want to know you're real, you're certified, and you can hold a delivery date. In ten
seconds they should see the plant, the SQF and Organic and Kosher marks, and the trucks.

**The buyer who already knows what they want.**

They want item number 2810501, case pack, unit weight — now, not after a phone call. They get
a sortable item sheet they can filter and export, and a quote request that lands in your inbox
already itemized.

Same website. It knows which one is looking.

Pricing stays private. Buyers request an account; you decide what they see.

---

## Door two — Candidates

**This is the part most distributors miss.**

You're hiring drivers, warehouse crew, packers, customer service and accounting — in North
Jersey, against Amazon and every other warehouse within twenty miles. The people you want have
options.

Before anyone applies, they look you up. Today they find a company that looks smaller and older
than it is.

A careers page changes that in an afternoon:

- Real photographs of the floor, the line, the trucks. Not stock photos.
- What each job actually is — shift, what you'll be doing, what it pays.
- Apply from a phone in under two minutes. Most of your candidates have no desktop computer.
- Spanish alongside English.
- Applications arrive sorted and searchable, not buried in an inbox.

**Why this matters commercially:** every week a truck sits idle for want of a driver costs more
than this website. Every packer who quits at ninety days costs you the hiring and the training
twice. Looking like a serious employer is the cheapest recruiting spend there is.

---

## Door three — Your own people

**A staff login, same address.**

Your team signs in and sees the operations system: live inventory, open orders, today's packing
assignments, pallets and manifests, delivery status.

No separate app. No desktop program that only runs on certain machines. It opens on a phone in
the warehouse, a tablet on the packing line, or a laptop in the office.

The tools your people already use every day, reached the same way everyone else reaches you.

---

## What it costs you to do nothing

- Buyers who compare you to a competitor with a better website and never call.
- Job openings that stay open, and overtime paid to cover them.
- Staff re-keying orders that arrived by email because there's nowhere else for them to go.
- A first impression that undersells a plant you've spent decades building.
